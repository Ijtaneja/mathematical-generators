Compare the classical proper-divisor sum with two digit-split constructions using powers or products. In every mode, check whether one number leads to another and back.

Three ways to form a two-way relationship

Classical: N → M = s(N) → N = s(M)

s(N) is the sum of positive proper divisors. For example, s(220) = 284 and s(284) = 220.

Power: N = a|b → M = ap ± bp → N = ±dp ± ep

Example: 240 = 2|40 → 2² + 40² = 1604 → 16² − 04² = 240.

Product: N = a|b → M = k(a ± b) → N = k(d ± e)

Example: 150 = 1|50 → 5(1 + 50) = 255 → 5(25 + 5) = 150.

Complete original 300625 example

300625 = 300|625 → 3002 − 6252 = −300625
−300625 = (−300)|625 → −(−300)2 + 6252 = 300625

Here 300² = 90,000 and 625² = 390,625, so both steps are exact. The parentheses around −300 make the second step unambiguous.

Here a|b means a cut between digits, not division. The second cut is M = d|e. Classical amicable numbers use proper-divisor sums; the Power and Product modes apply the two-way idea to digit blocks. The intermediate M may be negative; its minus sign belongs to its first block.
