Orders 3–12 · Normal, pandiagonal and structured constructions

Order

Arrangements use the same prime set in different positions. Each result is checked against the selected design. They are not disjoint sets of primes.

Verified prime squares

Search for a new prime set

Try a common positive even shift of the selected starting example. Adding the same value preserves the design’s sum equalities; a new set is accepted only when every entry is prime. A bounded search may find no new set.

Mathematical requirements

Every complete square has distinct prime entries and equal rows, columns and both principal diagonals. Pandiagonal types additionally satisfy every wrapping diagonal. Perfect Pandiagonal order 4 also checks all wrapping 2×2 blocks.

Normal and Pandiagonal: orders 4–8 each have separate options; further orders retain their named construction variants. Normal requires equal rows, columns and the two principal diagonals; Pandiagonal additionally requires every wrapping diagonal.

For order 5, only Concentric requires a fully magic central 3×3. Cornered and Semi-Striped require equal rows and columns in their 3×3 corner, without a diagonal requirement. Cyclic-Border has four rotated magic rectangles around a central prime.

Orders 9–12 include verified prime constructions with their listed component properties. Every available choice has a verified prime starting example. The order-11 Pandiagonal example is credited to Jarek Wroblewski (AZsPCs 2013 final report).

Order 8 is divided into Type 1 (eight designs) and Type 2: Striped (five striped designs). Its Concentric design preserves both 4×4 and 6×6 magic cores; its Pandiagonal design contains four equal-sum 4×4 magic blocks. Other listed rectangles retain exactly the row, column and diagonal requirements shown in the checks.

Order 7 adds ten types with the required cores, rectangles and wrapping-diagonal checks. Its Pandiagonal starting example (Jarek Wroblewski, OEIS A179440) uses primes 3–239 and magic sum 733. The Normal starting example rearranges the same primes.

Order 6 adds Normal, Pandiagonal, Concentric, Cornered, Border-Nested, Striped-1, Striped-2 and Striped-3. Its 4×4 cores are fully magic in the Concentric and Cornered designs; the 2×2 corner is not required to be magic.

The order-5 Cornered example’s extended 5×2 and 2×5 regions preserve equal columns or equal rows respectively; they are not labelled fully magic rectangles. Exact component sums and diagonal checks appear beneath every square.

The compact order-5 Normal example uses the 25 consecutive primes 13–113 and magic sum 313 (OEIS A272386). The compact order-5 Pandiagonal example uses primes 5–227 and magic sum 395 (V. Pavlovsky, OEIS A179440). The order-6 Pandiagonal example has magic sum 450 and primes 3–167 (Radko Nachev, OEIS A179440). The optional prime-progression mode uses PrimeGrid’s AP26: 43142746595714191 + k × 5283234035979900, k = 0,…,24. Progression reference. Exact integer arithmetic and deterministic Miller–Rabin bases are used for integers below 2⁶⁴. Larger candidates are rejected. Excel stores integers with more than 15 digits as text to preserve their exact values. The progression offers 18 overlapping sets for order 3, 11 for order 4 and 2 for order 5.
