Understanding the mathematics and the colors

Within each family, colors follow positions in its seed block. Repeated occurrences of the same digit can therefore have different colors. Every displayed multiplier applies to the original repeating block of 1/n, including the products in other families.

A rotation moves digits from one end of the block to the other without changing their cyclic order. The repeating blocks of k/n fall into families under these shifts. When k and n have a common factor, a shorter block may repeat inside the displayed block.

If B is the integer represented by the repeating block and L is its length, then n × B = 10L − 1: a number consisting of L nines. This identity is displayed below the family panels.
