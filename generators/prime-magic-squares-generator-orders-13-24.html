Orders 13–24 · Normal, pandiagonal and structured constructions

Order

Arrangements use the same prime set in different positions. Each result is checked against the selected design. They are not disjoint sets of primes.

Verified prime squares

Search for a new prime set

Try a common positive even shift of the selected starting example. Adding the same value preserves the design’s sum equalities; a new set is accepted only when every entry is prime. A bounded search may find no new set.

Mathematical requirements

Every available design has distinct prime entries and equal complete rows, columns and both principal diagonals. General Pandiagonal additionally checks every wrapping diagonal. Compound designs check every listed internal magic square and their different block sums. Concentric designs check their listed inner magic squares.

This first version covers all orders 13–24. General Pandiagonal examples for orders 13–20 are credited to Jarek Wroblewski in the AZsPCs 2013 final report; their Normal options rearrange the same primes. The compound examples for orders 20 and 21 and the concentric examples for every order 13–24 were constructed and checked for this generator. Additional sequential-generator layouts have not yet been converted to primes.

Arrangements reuse the selected prime set. The bounded new-prime search applies a common even shift and accepts it only if every entry remains prime. It may find no new set. Exact integer arithmetic is used below 2⁶⁴; larger candidates are rejected. Excel stores integers longer than 15 digits as text.
